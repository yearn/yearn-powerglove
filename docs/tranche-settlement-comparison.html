Weekly settlement increases earnings by $1,383.78 (53.7%) and cumulative return by 0.554 percentage points. Equity absorbs $3,080.18 of shortfalls across 16 daily settlements versus $1,354.13 across two weekly settlements. Its allocated excess also decreases, from $5,656.53 to $5,314.27.

Method

The same frozen daily underlying PPS and inferred Senior/Junior cash flows are replayed for both cases. Initial capital, target rates, excess allocation, loss priority, fees and zero profit-unlock delay are unchanged. The independent weekly replay matches every frozen economic NAV. This comparison assumes zero profit-unlock delay and isolates settlement frequency.

Weekly settlement is anchored on August 1: August 8, August 15, and every seven days through October 31, followed by a final one-day settlement on November 1. This gives 14 settlements versus 92 daily settlements. Each settlement is followed immediately by reports for all tranches.

Accrual follows controller interaction timing: settlements accrue all tranches; daily deposits and withdrawals accrue the affected tranche. Flows are priced at the current target-accrued claim, without allocating unsettled excess. There is no extra daily settlement hidden in the weekly run.

Interpretation

Longer intervals let some low-return days offset high-return days before applying the asymmetric waterfall. Equity bears shortfalls first but receives only 20% of positive excess. The observed benefit is specific to this return path and weekly anchor; it is not a guarantee that less frequent settlement always improves Equity returns.

This is an economic simulation, not deployed vault share-price performance.
